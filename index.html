India's Smog Story: Air Pollution Awareness

India's struggle with air pollution is glaringly evident in cities like Delhi and Mumbai, where the Air Quality Index (AQI) often reaches alarming levels — particularly during winter months. This toxic mix of vehicular emissions, industrial activity, and seasonal crop burning blankets urban centers in thick smog, worsening respiratory health for millions of residents.

Air Quality Index (AQI) Reference Guide

AQI Range Air Pollution Level Health Implications Cautionary Statement (PM2.5)
0 – 50 Good Air quality is satisfactory; pollution poses little or no risk. None
51 – 100 Moderate Acceptable quality; sensitive individuals may experience minor effects. Active children and adults with respiratory conditions should limit prolonged outdoor exertion.
101 – 150 Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups Sensitive groups may experience health effects; the general public is unlikely to be affected. People with respiratory conditions should limit prolonged outdoor exertion.
151 – 200 Unhealthy Everyone may begin to experience health effects; sensitive groups may experience more serious effects. People with respiratory conditions should avoid prolonged outdoor exertion; others should limit it.
201 – 300 Very Unhealthy Health alert; the entire population is more likely to be affected. Everyone should avoid all outdoor exertion; children should remain indoors.
300+ Hazardous Health emergency — everyone may experience serious health effects. Everyone must avoid all outdoor exertion.
